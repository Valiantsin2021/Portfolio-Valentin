Where agents sit in my QA team's processes

I led a team to implement autonomous agents across our QA processes. Agents with project and product knowledge take the high-volume work while people make the calls that need judgement.

  1. PnPlan

    Requirements analysis & refinement

    AI-assisted requirements analysis implemented with human in the loop, grooming and refinement. Ambiguities, gaps and testability risks come up during refinement, while they are still cheap to fix.

  2. AAssemble

    Test design & automation

  3. IInspect

How I run AI adoption

I started in QA in 2018 as a manual tester. That's where I learned where testing time really goes, and it's why I moved into automation, then SDET work, then leading a team. Now I manage how we use AI in testing. Having done each of those jobs, I know which parts are repetitive enough to give to an agent. Triage, sorting through test results and the first read of new requirements go to agents, while the decisions stay with people. I measure a baseline before I introduce anything, because if the gain can't show up as a number on the report, I don't trust it.

  1. DDefinePick the repetitive work an agent can take
  2. MMeasureBaseline the manual effort
  3. AAnalyzeFind where the testing time really goes
  4. IImproveIntroduce the agent
  5. CControlControl the result

Measure first

I am Lean Six Sigma Green Belt certified. Each agent developed in my team follows DMAIC: baseline the manual effort, introduce the agent, then control the result.

People in the loop

I follow the approach where agents absorb the repetitive work. Analysts move up to exploratory, risk-based testing and to reviewing what the agents produce.

Enable the team

I led 10+ internal trainings a year on current automation and AI practices, and hands-on mentoring for analysts moving into agentic automation. Four team members certified in Playwright and Langchain.

One stack, one standard

I consolidated four insurance and underwriting projects onto a single automated framework I managed my team to build from scratch. That removed tools spread and made quality comparable across projects.
